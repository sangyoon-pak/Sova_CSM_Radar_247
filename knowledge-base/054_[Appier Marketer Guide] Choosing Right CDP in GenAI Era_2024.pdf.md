Implementing a CDP is a multifaceted initiative that impacts multiple parts of an organization, extending well beyond the marketing department alone. Early identification and engagement of key stakeholders are crucial to align strategic objectives, secure comprehensive buy-in, and ensure that the CDP delivers optimal value across the enterprise. Here is a systematic approach to stakeholder identification: 
1.  Assessing the Main Business Benefits of CDPs 
Revenue Enhancement: Key stakeholders include the Chief Marketing Officer (CMO), Chief Revenue Officer (CRO), and finance department representatives. Their focus is on how the CDP can enhance metrics such as customer lifetime value (CLV), return on ad spend (ROAS), and overall revenue growth. 
Cost Reduction: Involves stakeholders like the Chief Financial Officer (CFO), Chief Operating Officer (COO), and heads of marketing operations and customer support, who are concerned with operational efficiency and cost management. Their focus is on the CDP’ s potential to streamline processes, reduce dependency on costly third-party data vendors, and decrease manual labor. 
Time Efficiency: Stakeholders such as marketing and data analytics teams benefit from a CDP that facilitates self-service access to data, automated reporting, and efficient segmentation, which frees them to focus on strategic activities rather than manual data handling. 
Risk Mitigation: This includes the Chief Information Security Officer (CISO), legal counsel, and data privacy officers, who ensure that the CDP complies with data privacy laws like GDPR and CCPA, secures customer data, and minimizes legal risks.
